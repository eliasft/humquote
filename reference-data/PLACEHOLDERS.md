# Placeholders, assumptions and the research to-do list

Everything marked placeholder below is a stand-in, flagged in the web versions and
the Streamlit app wherever it affects a price. Replace each one in the file named,
then remove it from this list.

`reference-data/tariffs.json` is generated by `reference-data/make_tariffs.py`, which
holds every rate in the units of the network's price list. To change a tariff, edit
the script and run `python reference-data/make_tariffs.py` from the repo root; the
next deploy picks it up (no code changes needed).

## Research scope (decided 14 Nov 2026)

- **Customers:** C&I sites, not heavy industry: LV business tariffs and HV demand
  tariffs, not subtransmission or transmission connections.
- **States:** HUM operates in NSW, QLD, VIC and SA, mainly South East Queensland and
  northern NSW. **NSW and QLD first** (done 14 Nov 2026), then VIC and SA.
- **Network charges are passed through at cost** in HUM's contracts. The quote's
  network component is indicative; the modelling simplifications below are accepted
  as the quoter's judgement.
- **Customer profiles:** the apps quote clients of any profile (consumption, load
  factor, time-of-use shares, voltage). The default profile (400 MWh, 50% peak, 0%
  shoulder, load factor 0.55, LV) stays as it is.

## 1. Network tariffs: NSW and QLD researched (FY27)

Source: each network's published 2026-27 network price list and tariff guide (named

(the first one that fits the site's voltage and annual consumption):

| Network | LV small business | LV large | HV |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ausgrid | EA225 (up to 100 MWh) | EA302 (100-160), EA305 (160-750), EA310 (750+) | EA370 |
| Endeavour Energy | N91 (up to 160 MWh) | N19 (160+) | N29 |
| Essential Energy | BLNBSS1 (up to 160 MWh); BLNT1AO (closed to new sites) | BLND3AO (160+); BLND3TO (alternative) | BHND3AO |
| Energex | 6800 (up to 100 MWh) | 7200 (100+); 8300, 94300 (optional) | 7400 (line); 4000 (dedicated bus) |
| Ergon Energy (East zone) | EBTOUET1 (up to 100 MWh) | ELTOUDT1 (100+); EDSTT1, ELTOUET1 (optional) | EC22LT1 |

**What changed from the old presets.** The four "verified" presets came from the old
HUM.py and were FY26 or older rates (Energex 8300 matches Energex's 2025-26 price list
exactly). Energex 8100 no longer exists (replaced by 7200); BLNT1AO is closed to new
sites (BLNBSS1 is the default); BLND3AO is for sites over 160 MWh, not 100, and has
**demand charges** (peak 12.0390, shoulder 10.8924, off-peak 2.8541 $/kVA/month), which
the old preset set to zero. Saved quotes and share links that use a replaced tariff
open on its successor and keep their own rates.

**Modelling simplifications** (also listed on each tariff in the apps, under its time
windows):

- **Demand in several windows is summed.** The model has one demand charge on the
  site's monthly peak demand, so a tariff that charges demand separately in its peak,
  shoulder and off-peak windows gets the sum of those rates. This assumes the site's
  peak demand occurs in every window, which is conservative. **Decision for HUM:** keep
  the sum, or count only the peak window (or peak and shoulder). On the default quote
  (NSW, BLND3AO): sum of all three $0.2846/kWh; peak and shoulder $0.2775; peak only
  $0.2504; no demand charge $0.2204 (the old preset gave $0.2127).
- Daily rates (c/kVA/day) are converted at 365/12 days a month; Endeavour's seasonal
  rates are averaged by month (high season Nov-Mar, low season Apr-Oct); per-kW demand
  rates (Ausgrid EA302) are used as per kVA.
- Tariffs with no shoulder period charge the shoulder share at the off-peak rate;
  Endeavour N91's solar soak rate (10am-2pm) takes the shoulder slot.
- Not included: connection unit charges (HV, site-specific); Ergon's West and Mt Isa
  pricing zones (East only); Energex 94300's 120 kVA minimum demand.
- Fixed charges include the network's metering charge where it is listed separately.

**Confirmed by HUM (14 Nov 2026):** Ausgrid EA302's demand charge applies all year.

- Ausgrid's time windows come from Ausgrid's website and retailers' summaries; the

## 6. Historical network charges for the index (done 14 Nov 2026)

Each index date now uses the network rates in force on it: the reference tariffs'
`history` in the catalogue (`HISTORY` in `make_tariffs.py`) holds FY22 to FY26 rates
from each year's published price list, and FY27 is the current catalogue. Sources: the
AER's annual pricing pages (FY22-FY24 price lists for both networks; Energex FY25) and
the networks' own websites (Essential FY25 and FY26; Energex FY26).

- **Essential BLND3AO**: same structure every year (time-of-use energy, demand in each
  of the three windows, summed as for FY27).
- **Energex 7200**: until FY25 it had one volume rate and charged demand in the peak
  window plus "excess demand" above it at other times. With the model's single demand
  (the same in every window) the excess is zero, so only the peak-window rate counts.
  From FY26 it has time-of-use energy and peak and shoulder demand, as in FY27. The FY22
  and FY23 price lists show no daily charge for 7200, only ACS capital metering.
- **VIC and SA** (placeholders) and every other tariff use FY27 rates on every date.

Effect on the index (average over each year's dates, $/kWh, new less FY27-on-every-date):
NSW FY22 -0.032, FY23 -0.030, FY24 -0.026, FY25 -0.020, FY26 -0.009; QLD FY22 -0.011,
FY23 -0.019, FY24 -0.012, FY25 -0.002, FY26 -0.011.

## Keeping the tariffs up to date (each 1 July)

Networks publish next year's prices once the AER approves them (late May or early June),
effective 1 July; some reissue a list mid-year (Energex's "updated for Sch 8"). Each year:

1. **Keep this year's rates for the index.** In `make_tariffs.py`, add a `HISTORY` entry
   for the year ending (e.g. `'FY27'`) for each index reference tariff, copying its
   current figures. Do this before overwriting them.
2. **Enter the new price lists.** For each researched network, update the figures in
   `make_tariffs.py` (they are in the price list's own units, so each can be checked
   line by line), the `SOURCES` names, and `financialYear`.
3. **Check codes and eligibility.** Tariffs get renamed, closed or re-thresholded (FY27
   retired Energex 8100; BLNT1AO closed). Add a removed id to `retired` (its successor),
   and update eligibility bounds, `periods` and `notes`.
4. **Regenerate and test.** `python reference-data/make_tariffs.py`, then
   `python app-b/test/generate_core_cases.py` and `npm test` in `app-b`. Tests that pin
   specific rates (core.test.ts) need the new figures.
5. **Check the apps** (A, B and Streamlit): the default quote and the index's latest
   point, and that no new tariff is flagged as not fitting the default profile.

## Not placeholders, but worth knowing

- **Quarterly and calendar-year contracts** are collected from 13 Nov 2026 onwards
  (`futures_strips`). Before then only FY strips exist, so historical quotes and the
  index fill months not covered by an FY strip by back-filling or carrying forward,
  and flag those dates.
- **Quarter averaging**: a 12-month period without a matching annual strip averages
  its quarters weighted by days. A supply start part-way through a quarter (any month
  can be chosen) gives a part quarter at each end, each weighted by its days.
