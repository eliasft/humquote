# Placeholders, assumptions and the research to-do list

Everything marked placeholder below is a stand-in, flagged in the web versions and
the Streamlit app wherever it affects a price. Replace each one in the file named,
then remove it from this list.

`reference-data/tariffs.json` is generated by `reference-data/make_tariffs.py`, which
holds every rate in the units of the network's price list. To change a tariff, edit
the script and run `python reference-data/make_tariffs.py` from the repo root; the
next deploy picks it up (no code changes needed).

## Research scope (decided 14 Nov 2026)

  tariffs, not subtransmission or transmission connections.

|---|---|---|---|

| Energex | 6800 (up to 100 MWh) | 7200 (100+); 8300, 94300 (optional) | 7400 (line); 4000 (dedicated bus) |
| Ergon Energy (East zone) | EBTOUET1 (up to 100 MWh) | ELTOUDT1 (100+); EDSTT1, ELTOUET1 (optional) | EC22LT1 |

**What changed from the old presets.** The four "verified" presets came from the old
HUM.py and were FY26 or older rates (Energex 8300 matches Energex's 2025-26 price list
exactly). Energex 8100 no longer exists (replaced by 7200); BLNT1AO is closed to new
sites (BLNBSS1 is the default); BLND3AO is for sites over 160 MWh, not 100, and has
**demand charges** (peak 12.0390, shoulder 10.8924, off-peak 2.8541 $/kVA/month), which
the old preset set to zero. Saved quotes and share links that use a replaced tariff
open on its successor and keep their own rates.

**Modelling simplifications** (also listed on each tariff in the apps, under its time
windows):

- **Demand charges are priced as the bill shows them** (decided 14 Nov 2026). Each
  tariff lists its demand lines (`demand.lines`: label, window, rate, and whether the
  kVA is the month's maximum, the 12-month maximum or authorised demand). A quote has
  one rate per line and two ways to price them:
  - **Estimate** (default): every line on the estimated demand, annual kWh ÷ 8,760 ÷
    load factor. This assumes the site's maximum occurs in every window, which is
    conservative for sites with lower evening or overnight demand. On the default
    quote this gives $0.2846/kWh, the same as before.
  - **Client's bill**: each line's kVA from the bill (the average of the monthly
    figures on the last 12 bills; for Ausgrid's capacity charge, the 12-month maximum),
    and the kWh in the network's peak, shoulder and off-peak windows (`energyWindows`),
    which then split the annual consumption for network energy charges. ASX energy
    still uses the consumption shares. A daytime business at 70% / 100% / 40% of its
    maximum in Essential's peak / shoulder / off-peak windows prices at about
    $0.2713/kWh.
  Bill kVA includes the site's power factor, which the estimate doesn't.
- Daily rates (c/kVA/day) are converted at 365/12 days a month; Endeavour's seasonal
  rates are averaged by month (high season Nov-Mar, low season Apr-Oct); per-kW rates
  (Ausgrid EA302's capacity charge) are used as per kVA.
- Tariffs with no shoulder period charge the shoulder share at the off-peak rate;
  Endeavour N91's solar soak rate (10am-2pm) takes the shoulder slot.
- Not included: connection unit charges (HV, site-specific); Ergon's West and Mt Isa
  pricing zones (East only).
- Fixed charges include the network's metering charge where it is listed separately.

**Confirmed 16 Nov 2026** (nothing left in `toConfirm` for NSW and QLD):

- **Ausgrid EA302 charges all year.** Its 48.3397 c/kW/day is in the price list's
  *Network Capacity Prices: Peak (c/kW/day)* column: a capacity charge on the highest
  kW from 3pm to 9pm on working weekdays over the previous 12 months, charged every
  day. The summer/winter-only charge is the *High Season Peak* demand charge, which
  EA302 doesn't have (small business EA256 does). So no 8/12 factor: the rate stays as
  it was, now labelled a 12-month capacity charge (on a bill quote, enter the 12-month
  maximum kW). Sources: Ausgrid Network Price List 2026-27, p1; Ausgrid 2026-27 Pricing
  Proposal, Attachment A Statement of compliance, section 3.1.
- **Ausgrid's time windows**, from the same statement of compliance (section 3.1): peak
  energy 3pm-9pm on working weekdays (business) in summer (Nov-Mar) and winter
  (Jun-Aug), off-peak at all other times; capacity on the 3pm-9pm working-weekday
  window in any month. These match the catalogue.
- **Ergon ELTOUET1 (Large TOU Energy)** is optional, on request, for sites with a smart
  meter that used under 160 MWh in the previous 12 months **and** have monthly demand
  over 120 kVA; Ergon reviews it each year (AER final decision on the 2025-30 tariff
  structure statements, Attachment 19, section 19.4.5.1; Ergon's amended 2025-30 TSS,
  May 2026, footnote 14). Energex 94300 has the same rule. Both now carry
  `eligibility.minKva: 120`, and the apps flag them as not fitting when the site's
  estimated demand (consumption / 8,760 h / load factor) is 120 kVA or less. Under
  160 MWh, that takes a load factor below about 0.15, so few sites qualify.

## 2. VIC and SA tariffs: Powercor and SA Power Networks researched (FY27)

Sources: Powercor 2026-27 Pricing (7 May 2026) and its 2026-27 Tariff Summary workbook,
sheet NUOS (DUoS + TUoS + JSA, excl. GST); SA Power Networks Annual Pricing Proposal
Overview 2026-27 (July 2026), Table 9 (NUoS tariff schedule). 10 tariffs:

| Network | Small business | Medium | LV large | HV |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Powercor | NDTOU (up to 40 MWh) | NDM (40-160), NDMO21 (optional) | LLV1/2/3 (160+) | HV1/2/3 |
| SA Power Networks | SBTOU (up to 160 MWh) | MBTOUD (40-160, over 120 kVA) | LBAD (160+), LBMD (optional) | HVAD |

**Powercor.** LLV1/2/3 and HV1/2/3 have identical prices (they differ only in the
three-hour incentive-demand window assigned to each zone substation), so each is one
tariff. Large tariffs charge a 12-month rolling maximum kVA (7am-7pm workdays) plus an
incentive demand in four months of the year; the incentive rate is entered as the
average over 12 months (rate x 4/12). HV has a 500 kVA minimum chargeable rolling
demand, not modelled. NDM's demand is in $/kW/month (summer Dec-Mar 23.11, non-summer
10.20, averaged) and used as per kVA. The NUOS schedule has no daily charge for the
large tariffs and no metering charge for any tariff, so metering is not included.

**SA Power Networks.** Rates are the non-CBD codes (CBD codes have the same prices).
Large tariffs charge two demands over the last 12 months: peak (highest daily average
5pm-9pm every day, Nov-Mar, billed all year) and anytime (highest 30-minute kVA), in
$/kVA/day (converted at 365/12 days). LBMD bills the peak demand in Nov-Mar only
(rate x 5/12). The 0.0262 $/day meter charge is in the fixed charge where listed.
SBTOU's peak applies Nov-Mar only (no peak in Apr-Oct), as the price list says; the
quote uses the peak rate on the peak share the user enters. Not included: flexible and
generation tariffs, HVAD500 (annual demand under 500 kVA), HVMD, and individually
calculated site charges (LBAD305 and similar codes).

**Other Victorian networks** (CitiPower, Jemena, AusNet Services, United Energy) still
have three placeholder entries each (LV business demand from 100 MWh, LV business
time-of-use up to 160 MWh, HV demand), status `placeholder`, code `TBC`, copying the
rates of Essential's BLND3AO, BLNBSS1 and BHND3AO. Research them the same way: add each
network to `make_tariffs.py` from its FY27 price list.

## 6. Historical network charges for the index (done 14 Nov 2026)

Each index date now uses the network rates in force on it: the reference tariffs'
`history` in the catalogue (`HISTORY` in `make_tariffs.py`) holds FY22 to FY26 rates
from each year's published price list, and FY27 is the current catalogue. Sources: the
AER's annual pricing pages (FY22-FY24 price lists for both networks; Energex FY25) and
the networks' own websites (Essential FY25 and FY26; Energex FY26).

- **Essential BLND3AO**: same structure every year (time-of-use energy, demand in each
  of the three windows, summed as for FY27).
- **Energex 7200**: until FY25 it had one volume rate and charged demand in the peak
  window plus "excess demand" above it at other times. With the model's single demand
  (the same in every window) the excess is zero, so only the peak-window rate counts.
  From FY26 it has time-of-use energy and peak and shoulder demand, as in FY27. The FY22
  and FY23 price lists show no daily charge for 7200, only ACS capital metering.
- **VIC and SA** (Powercor LLV, SA Power Networks LBAD: FY22-FY26 price lists not yet entered) and every
  other tariff use FY27 rates on every date.

Effect on the index (average over each year's dates, $/kWh, new less FY27-on-every-date):
NSW FY22 -0.032, FY23 -0.030, FY24 -0.026, FY25 -0.020, FY26 -0.009; QLD FY22 -0.011,
FY23 -0.019, FY24 -0.012, FY25 -0.002, FY26 -0.011.

## Keeping the tariffs up to date (each 1 July)

Networks publish next year's prices once the AER approves them (late May or early June),
effective 1 July; some reissue a list mid-year (Energex's "updated for Sch 8"). Each year:

1. **Keep this year's rates for the index.** In `make_tariffs.py`, add a `HISTORY` entry
   for the year ending (e.g. `'FY27'`) for each index reference tariff, copying its
   current figures. Do this before overwriting them.
2. **Enter the new price lists.** For each researched network, update the figures in
   `make_tariffs.py` (they are in the price list's own units, so each can be checked
   line by line), the `SOURCES` names, and `financialYear`.
3. **Check codes and eligibility.** Tariffs get renamed, closed or re-thresholded (FY27
   retired Energex 8100; BLNT1AO closed). Add a removed id to `retired` (its successor),
   and update eligibility bounds, `periods` and `notes`.
4. **Regenerate and test.** `python reference-data/make_tariffs.py`, then
   `python app-b/test/generate_core_cases.py` and `npm test` in `app-b`. Tests that pin
   specific rates (core.test.ts) need the new figures.
5. **Check the apps** (A, B and Streamlit): the default quote and the index's latest
   point, and that no new tariff is flagged as not fitting the default profile.

## Not placeholders, but worth knowing

- **What the futures history holds** (`data/asx_futures.csv`): calendar-year strips
  from Sep 2021 to Jun 2026 (ASX's old homepage listed calendar years), financial-year
  strips from 22 Jun 2026, and every quarter and yearly strip from 13 Nov 2026 (and on
  22 Jul 2026, recovered from an archived copy of ASX's page). Before 13 Nov 2026 there
  are no quarters, so historical quotes and the index fill months not covered by a strip
  by back-filling or carrying forward, and flag those dates. CY prices for the Jun–Nov
  2026 gap are estimated from the FY strips in `data/asx_futures_estimates.csv`, for the
  charts only (see `data/README.md`).
- **Quarter averaging**: a 12-month period without a matching annual strip averages
  its quarters weighted by days. A supply start part-way through a quarter (any month
  can be chosen) gives a part quarter at each end, each weighted by its days.
